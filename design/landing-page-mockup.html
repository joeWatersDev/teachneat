Round two

Logo directions, faithful shots, and a copy pass

Logo directions

Four marks, each shown at 64px, 32px (the header lockup) and 16px (a browser tab). Small sizes are where marks fail, so they are worth judging first.

A. Protractor toolkit Selected

The pocket-knife idea, with the protractor doing the work of the red handle. A pencil and a ruler fan out of it. At 16px it holds up as a gold arc with two blades above, which is the shape a pocket knife makes anyway.

Cleanup applied. The body was an ellipse, on which equal angles are not equal arcs, which is why the outer ticks sat closer together. It is now a true semicircle, so the five ticks are evenly spaced by construction rather than by eye. Ticks are also drawn inward from the rim between 0.86 and 0.66 of the radius, so the middle one can no longer cross the bottom edge.

B. Literal pocket knife

Closest to your description: a handle with a ruler, a pencil and a set square fanned out. It is the most legible at 64px and the most obviously a pocket knife. It is also the busiest, and 16px is where the third tool starts to close up.

C. Crossed pencil and ruler

The toolkit idea reduced to two implements in a tile. Reads cleanly at every size and sits comfortably next to the wordmark. The trade is that crossed tools are a common shorthand, so it is the least distinctive of the four.

D. The class

Last round's mark, kept for comparison. Five students with one picked out. It is about who the product serves rather than what it is called, which is why it does not fit the brief this time.

A is now the mark, applied in the header, the hero and the app shots. B, C and D are kept above only so the comparison survives in the record.

The shots are rebuilt against the real components

Last round I approximated. This round I read each component and matched it. What changed:

  • Attendance was wrong. I had drawn a coloured grid. Daily entry is actually a row per student with four square buttons, P, A, T and E, where the chosen one takes the gold fill, plus an optional note field. That is now what the panel shows.
  • Roster rows use the real six-column layout and the real accommodation tags, which are short codes such as AT, PS and NTA in gold pills, not the words. The avatar is the same outline icon the app uses, not initials.
  • Group cards match: a gold header bar with reorder chevrons, then a plain list of names. I previously had names as chips, which the app does not do.
  • The wheel uses the app's own eight colours from the rainbow scheme, and the same black-and-white pointer.
  • Email is the recipient table with its three real columns and checkboxes, not a compose window with placeholder chips.
  • Buttons, selects and segmented controls are drawn from the real primitives: 36px tall, 6px radius, gold fill for primary, bordered elevated for secondary.

These are still simplified. Fewer rows than a real class, no scrollbars, and the sidebar only appears in the hero. But nothing on the page now shows a control that does not exist or a layout the app does not use, so signing up should not feel like a different product.

Round three changes

  • Nothing claims a tool count any more. You flagged the hero paragraph, and the same problem sat in two headings that were counting out loud. The showcase heading is now "Every tool, one class list", and the hero headline has since been replaced outright.
  • The trial line no longer reads as a threat. "Your first 30 days include everything" could be heard as everything going away afterwards. It now says which 30 days are Premium and what remains after: "Every Premium feature free for 30 days. No card needed, and your account stays free after that."
  • Email leads on its two real strengths. Choosing exactly who hears from you, and one message that arrives personal to each family. Neither sentence mentions a placeholder, a template or a recipient filter.
  • Stars are off the tool names. Every tool has Premium parts, so a star on three of five was drawing a distinction that does not exist. One note under the showcase carries it instead, which also settles flag 4 below: the star no longer marks a tool at all.
  • Accommodations are spelled out in both roster shots, on fewer students. The app uses short codes because a 150px column has no room for words; the shot widens that column, since a visitor has no idea what NTA means.
  • The hero roster runs off the bottom edge with a sixth student half-visible, so it reads as a real class rather than a list that happens to end.
  • Also: the demo roster lost its Bulk Add and Add Student buttons, the attendance segmented control no longer stretches the panel width, the Statistics star is gone, and the wheel is rebuilt with eight names on eight segments, each rotated to its own segment, with the pointer aimed inward.
  • The roster row now collapses properly. Its three flexible columns were minmax(0, …), which lets a track shrink to nothing, so name, class and email all vanished before any breakpoint fired. Below 620px of row width it becomes three columns instead: name over class on the left, then the risk and absence flags in a reserved column of their own, then the accommodation. Only the email drops, since it is the one field a teacher can do without at a glance. The flag and tag tracks are fixed and fractional rather than auto, because an auto track is sized per row: a student with no accommodation would otherwise let the icons slide right and break the column.
  • It uses a container query, not a media query. The hero shot and the showcase panel are very different widths at the same viewport, so a viewport breakpoint would always be wrong for one of them. Worth borrowing in the app, where the roster sits in a content area whose width depends on the sidebar state.

Copy pass

Every line rewritten with a marketing reader in mind rather than an engineering one. What went:

  • All em dashes. Replaced with commas, full stops, or a restructured sentence.
  • "Our servers", "capabilities", "narrow permissions". A teacher does not care where our servers are. They care that nobody can take their class list and that a principal will not block them. "Nothing to hand over" says the same thing in words a person would use.
  • The headline leads with the benefit. It is now "Less admin. More teaching.", which names what a teacher actually wants back. The original led with where files are stored, which is a reason to believe rather than a reason to care; the sub-line carries that instead.
  • Feature descriptions talk about the classroom. "See who is slipping before it becomes a problem" instead of "statistics and absence flags". "Where the class can see it is fair" instead of "uniform random selection".
  • The premium note stopped hedging. It now says a star shows where a subscription adds more, rather than explaining a badge convention.

Still flagged from last round

Flag 1. Type scale

Two display steps added above the app's 24px ceiling: 34 to 52px for the hero, 24 to 32px for section headings, both weight 600 with negative tracking. Marketing pages only. Worth adding to DESIGN_SYSTEM as such.

Flag 2. A fourth ground colour

A warm neutral for the hero and closing bands, so two full-width sections separate from the content between them without spending the accent on decoration.

Flag 3. 48px buttons

The app's 36px primary button reads as a form control at hero scale. Marketing only.

Flag 4. Withdrawn

This flagged the star taking on a second meaning by marking a whole tool. Removing the stars from the tool names resolves it: the star now appears once, on a note about Premium, and never as a marker on a feature. Its meaning inside the app is unchanged, so there is nothing left to approve.

What is next

Three flags left to confirm, all of them additive to DESIGN_SYSTEM rather than in conflict with it. On your word I will build this into the real pages, split pricing from FAQ, and update the specs. The signup upsell belongs to onboarding, so it stays out of this step.
